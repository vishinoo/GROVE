Ring

The single most useful screen in the app. Nobody ships a spec sheet with a twelve-pound ring, so rather than explain what it ought to send, Grove shows what yours actually sends — and "merged" is it telling you it saw the double signal and treated it as one press.

 

One thing to know

iOS will not let any app be woken from fully closed by a button press. Grove has to be running for your ring to reach it.

Backgrounded is fine. Locked is fine. Pocketed is fine.
Swiping Grove away turns the ring off until you open it again.

The limit

Said up front rather than discovered on a walk. It is the one thing about Grove that cannot be fixed, so it is the last thing you read before signing in.

Three tiers, cheapest first.

A reply that takes four seconds feels broken in your ear in a way it never did in a chat bubble, and the account model allows two of the good ones an hour. So a turn only climbs when the tier below genuinely cannot serve it.

Grove answers before it runs anything. A run can take ten seconds, and ten seconds of silence reads as a crash.

What it cannot do.

Constraints the product is shaped around, not gaps waiting to be filled.

Wake from closed

No iOS app can be woken from a fully terminated state by a button press. Grove has to be running — backgrounded, locked and pocketed are all fine. Force-quitting it turns the ring off until you open it again.

Stay quiet and stay alive

Holding the audio session is what makes Grove the now-playing app, and that is what lets the ring reach it. An idle session gets torn down, so the module renders a silent loop to hold its place.

Most likely cause of an App Store rejection in the project. Fine for personal builds and TestFlight.

Drive the glasses

There is nothing to drive. They are an ordinary Bluetooth headset and iOS handles the routing, which is why there is no Bluetooth code in the app at all. The one thing worth checking is whether they connected for playback only.
